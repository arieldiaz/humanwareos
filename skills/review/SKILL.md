# Review

Use after consequential work, a surprise, or a recurring review period.

1. Recover the original goal and completion conditions before judging the result.
2. Compare intended and observed outcome, cost, and strategic value from evidence rather than memory.
3. State the most important gap or surprise plainly.
4. Identify at most three candidates that may be durable lessons. Do not turn every observation into a rule.

Done when the gap is clear enough to improve the next decision. Invoke an independent challenge only when another perspective would add information, not as automatic ceremony.
